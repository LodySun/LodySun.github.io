Research

Future directions

My recent work suggests that some narrative relations may be more readily detectable in neural patterns during recall than during viewing. The directions below grow out of that observation.

1. How narrative relations are reconstructed in memory

Stories often plant a detail early that acquires its meaning much later. In my pilot analysis of a public naturalistic fMRI dataset (Chen et al., 2017), I found no reliable evidence of plant–payoff pattern similarity during viewing using the current analysis. During payoff recall, however, patterns in posterior medial cortex showed selective similarity to the corresponding earlier plant scene, consistent with reinstatement of its context.

I want to build on this in five ways. First, replication and generalisation across narratives and recall tasks, particularly in The Sixth Sense dataset (Zadbood et al., 2022), where a final twist changes the interpretation of earlier scenes. Groups with different access to the twist during viewing and recall provide a useful comparison, although the scene-cued recall task differs from the free recall used in the pilot.

Second, the timing of meaning: comparing relations whose significance is available when an event first occurs with those that become interpretable only after later information, and asking how this changes their representation during viewing and recall.

Third, the form a relation takes during viewing: studies of hippocampal representations show that overlapping memories can become more distinct with learning (Favila et al., 2016; Chanales et al., 2017), motivating a search for differentiation as well as similarity. Interregional coupling offers another possible measure.

Fourth, timing: EEG and MEG could help establish when context reinstatement occurs relative to the onset of verbal report.

Fifth, extending this approach to other narrative devices, including suspense–resolution, repetition with variation, conflict, and privileged access to information. These offer ways to ask how anticipation, recurring structure, and competing goals shape event representations and later remembering. I am particularly interested in “inside information”: situations in which the audience knows something a character does not, or has access to one character’s knowledge that others lack. Such asymmetries could connect narrative memory with theory of mind, allowing us to test how viewers track who knows what and whether those distinctions are preserved or reconstructed during recall.

2. Individual styles of remembering a story

People who watch the same film retell it differently. Some emphasise causal order, others emotional turning points, perceptual details, or broader themes. Word-timestamped recall transcripts from existing datasets offer a starting point for characterising this variation, by aligning recollections with the story’s event structure and using language-model measures to describe their semantic trajectories.

With repeated observations of the same people recalling different stories, I want to ask whether these tendencies are stable across narratives. I also want to test whether they relate to the strength of earlier-context reinstatement: for example, whether people who explicitly connect events causally when retelling show stronger reinstatement of the scenes those events depend on.

3. Returning to a context and relating two events

Non-linear narratives separate the order in which events are shown from the order in which they must be understood. A flashback may return the viewer to an established context, while another transition introduces a different timeline. In my pilot dataset, scene-specific reinstatement from viewing to recall was robust, even though the cross-event similarity analysis did not detect plant–payoff relations during viewing.

This motivates a further question: during viewing, do returns to a familiar narrative context reinstate earlier representations, while shifts to a new timeline promote more distinct event representations? Pattern completion and separation provide a theoretical framework for developing these predictions (Yassa & Stark, 2011), but distinguishing those mechanisms would require more than observing changes in similarity. Behavioral timeline reconstruction could help establish how viewers understand the temporal structure and relate it to the neural measures.

4. Longer horizon: meaning-making in mood disorders

A longer-term question is whether related processes contribute to difficulties in interpreting experience. Depression has been associated with more negative or less positive interpretations of ambiguous situations. It has also been associated with reduced specificity in autobiographical recall (Williams et al., 2007). These are distinct phenomena, and I am interested in how they interact: when a negative interpretation emerges, whether it changes how an event is later remembered, and how people organise accounts of their own past and imagined future.

Further ahead, I would like to investigate whether semantic features of psychotherapy dialogue track changes in interpretation and memory, and whether those changes relate to changes in brain function.

Achievements

Poster · GitHub repo

Reinstatement of narrative context during recall
Independent project, presented at Brain & Mind Symposium 2026

Asking whether the brain preserves long-range narrative relationships — plant–payoff pairs, where an inconspicuous early detail becomes meaningful only when it returns. A pair-resolved fMRI framework for the Sherlock dataset integrates viewing and free-recall representations with constrained permutation testing and time-resolved RSA. Plant–payoff structure was not detectable during viewing, but recalling a payoff selectively reinstated its own plant’s context in posterior medial cortex.

Poster · MSc thesis · GitHub repo

Staged rule acquisition in WishGLD
MSc thesis, University of Helsinki, supervised by Benjamin U. Cowley

Modeling rule learning as a temporally extended, multi-marker transition rather than a single event. A two-state hidden Markov model estimates trial-wise acquisition dynamics, compared against the first-correct response and a logical-collapse marker, and related to feedback-locked EEG (FRN, P3b) and inter-subject representational similarity (IS-RSA).

Cross-species attentional set-shifting
Research assistant, HiPerCog Group, University of Helsinki

Building the human-side analysis pipeline for a rat–human Go/NoGo attentional set-shifting paradigm: preprocessing from raw behavioral output through a model-fitting pipeline matched to the existing rodent pipeline, so that both species can be fitted under a single model.

BSc thesis

Locus coeruleus integrity and set-shifting in Parkinson's disease
BSc thesis, CAS Institute of Neuroscience, supervised by Zheng Ye

Quantified locus coeruleus / substantia nigra integrity from neuromelanin-MRI and linked these structural metrics to set-shifting deficits (CANTAB IED) in Parkinson's disease.
