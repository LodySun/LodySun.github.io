J. Sun. "When payoff returns: Reinstatement of earlier narrative context during recall." Brain & Mind Symposium, Helsinki, Oct. 2026. Poster · accepted Poster PDF ↗
Abstract

Stories often set up a plant whose meaning changes at a later payoff as they progress to keep the viewers engaged. We asked if such links appear as shared neural patterns during viewing, and how they are expressed during recall. We reanalyzed fMRI data from the Sherlock dataset (viewing and free recall; N = 17; Chen et al., 2017), using 11 pre-specified annotated plant-payoff pairs.

During viewing, posterior medial cortex (PMC) similarity between plants and payoffs did not exceed temporally matched controls (p = 0.568). During recall, PMC patterns for a recalled payoff resembled its own plant more than other earlier scenes (identity percentile 0.550 vs. null 0.482; q < 0.05). The effect survived controlling for the payoff’s own movie pattern, peaked ~4.5 s after recall onset, was absent in auditory cortex, and was not explained by naming the plant. Thus, long-range narrative links may become measurable when memory is reconstructed, rather than during viewing.

J. Sun, K. Lukander, and B. U. Cowley. "Staged rule acquisition in WishGLD: Dissociation and compression across learning." 9th International Conference on Motivational and Cognitive Control (MCC), Helsinki, Aug. 2026. Poster · accepted Poster PDF ↗
J. Sun. "Staged Rule Acquisition in WishGLD: Dissociation and Compression Across Learning." Master's thesis, University of Helsinki, 2026. Full text (HELDA) ↗
Abstract

Cognitive flexibility requires inferring changing task rules from feedback and stabilizing behavior around relevant rules. Wisconsin Card Sorting Test-like studies have used different markers of rule acquisition, leaving open whether acquisition is best treated as a single learning point or as a transition from search to stable rule use. Here, we examined rule learning in the Wisconsin-ish Global–Local Dissociation (WishGLD) task as a multi-marker process. Behavioral responses were modeled with a two-state hidden Markov model to estimate acquisition dynamics. Hidden Markov Model (HMM)-defined acquisition was compared with the first correct response and logical collapse, defined as the point at which feedback reduced the hypothesis space to a single rule-consistent candidate. Feedback-locked EEG responses were analyzed around these markers, focusing on FRN and P3b components.

The behavioral markers were related but separable: in early sets, first correct responses often preceded behavioral stabilization, whereas later sets showed stronger marker convergence. EEG results supported this dissociation. P3b showed a staged profile across acquisition, increasing at first correct feedback, decreasing during transition, and increasing again at the acquisition point, consistent with separable updating and stabilization operations. FRN-related activity was associated with HMM-derived transition metrics. IS-RSA further showed significant alignment between behavioral acquisition profiles and EEG feature profiles. These findings characterize rule acquisition in WishGLD as a temporally structured transition rather than a single learning point.
